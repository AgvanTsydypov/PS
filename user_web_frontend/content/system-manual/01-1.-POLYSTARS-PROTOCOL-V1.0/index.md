## 1.1 The Premise
Prediction markets aggregate private opinion into public price. A trader who believes a probability is mispriced commits capital against the consensus; the consensus shifts; the price moves. Multiplied across thousands of participants and resolved against real-world outcomes, the resulting record is a continuous, public, capital-weighted measurement of how a population thought about the future before the future arrived.

The record is not symmetric. Some participants timed entries early. Some committed heavy capital. Some exited cleanly at apex efficiency. Some entered at terminal certainty and harvested risk-free yield. Some held losing positions through resolution. Each behavior leaves a different mathematical signature on the resolved event, and each signature reflects a different way of being inside the system.

Polymarket currently serves as the venue with the largest cultural reach for these behaviors. Its events have produced the most legible record of this behavior: capital deployed into the U.S. presidential election cycle, into geopolitical resolution, into financial milestone bets, into the cultural moments that defined the period. The behavior is recorded on-chain. It is permanent. It is queryable. It is also, almost everywhere outside the platform itself, invisible; flattened into aggregate flow, lost into trade history, undifferentiated.

__Polystars surfaces it.__

## 1.2 The Collectible Stars of Prediction Markets

Polystars is a curated archive of behavioral records from resolved Polymarket events, expressed as collectible cards minted directly to the wallets that produced the underlying behavior. Each card refers to a specific event, a specific wallet, and a specific behavioral classification computed from the wallet's actual on-chain activity. The card is not metaphorical. It is the wallet's record on that event, made visible.

> The cards are Stars — the structurally significant participants in a system that otherwise only produces aggregate price discovery. The wallet that entered the U.S. presidential election market at 0.20 implied probability and held to resolution. The wallet that deployed $2 million at terminal certainty for half-a-cent yield. The wallet whose pattern across one event scaled in lockstep with the implied probability of every entry it made. These behaviors are rare, distinctive, and individually meaningful. Polystars makes them collectible.

The protocol classifies behavior into thirteen archetypes. Each card carries one. The classification is deterministic, computed from the wallet's measurable activity on the event, and bound to mathematical gates documented in Section 2. The card itself is a structured artifact. Front-face data, back-face dossier, encoded signature, archival visual surface documented in Section 3 onward. The archive accumulates seasonally, with 333 cards per Standard Season and a foundational set of 2,000 anchored to the Genesis Epoch.

## 1.3 The Landscape

Two things hold the contemporary attention surface with sustained presence rather than passing fascination: collectible cards as a format, and prediction markets as an information venue. Both have moved from speculative novelty into structural permanence within their respective domains. Both produce communities, holders, traders, completionists. Neither is a meta cycle; neither is expected to fade in the foreseeable future.

The intersection of the two is structurally underbuilt. Collectible cards have largely been speculative novelties or culture objects; prediction markets have been informational platforms without persistent collector culture. The space where the two combine—collectibles whose substance is the prediction record itself—has been mostly absent from the crypto-native landscape. Polystars exists in that space.

> This document specifies the protocol that produces the archive. The system is built. Whether the archive accumulates the cultural weight its mechanics are designed to support is a function of how the broader prediction-market and collectibles communities engage with it. The protocol cannot enforce significance. It can only provide the layer from which significance, if it forms, will be built.
